# 9. Visual brief and standalone image-generation prompts

## Identity and key art

**BOUNCE. EVERY FALL HAS A RHYTHM.**

Build the title as B + basketball + UNCE. The basketball replaces the only O; it is never an extra illustration above an otherwise complete word. Its visible diameter matches the cap height of B, U, N, C and E, with recognizable black seam lines. Use heavy, condensed lettering with open counters. Keep seams strong enough to survive a thumbnail. Test the mark in one color and at 160 pixels wide. The web wordmark is live type with the same photorealistic leather 3D ball in every logo, keeping spelling reliable.

The core world is charcoal, worn court timber, nighttime pavement and the warm orange of a working ball. Yellow isolates Isaiah. The other seven identities stay visible as their own colors: Lena orange, Dani blue, Andre/Velvet violet, Ben red, Jude green, Iris indigo, Morgan pearl white. Faces need neutral fill; colored lighting must not make people unreadable. The seven-chakra palette is a design foundation. BOUNCE's pearl-white Soul Star is a theatrical extension, not a statement that this full eight-part arrangement is a universal traditional system.

Isaiah belongs at the center of the key art, with his body grounded and the ball in his possession. Give the artists evidence of work: a rehearsal floor, an amplifier, a microphone, an unfinished painting, marked score pages, an audition script and drag costume construction. They are people pursuing their own work, not an admiring chorus around a savior. Preserve separate zones within a shared city. Avoid generic championship trophies, literal mystical diagrams, floating inspirational words, fake reviews and invented production credits.

The current website uses original generated concept art, not actual cast photography. It depicts a possible ensemble and environment. A production photoshoot would replace it once performers and designers are attached. The website poster is a composition study; final print key art should be generated or photographed specifically for a 2:3 crop so all seven supporting figures survive.

## Film language

Match the ball's drop to a heel lowering from relevé; match its return to Jude's hand coming off a string. A brush wipes across a painted face; a rehearsal mirror gives way to a cramped home window. The opening's color worlds intercut separate locations and do not imply that the eight have already met. Hold on Zay long enough to enjoy him before broadening the ensemble.

The cut to Denise’s Las Vegas home has no dissolving glow or emotional underscoring. Carry the journey through keys, a door, Uncle Ray’s car, an overnight flight and New York transit; the next evening’s bar encounter must never imply the cities are adjacent. A ball caught at chest height becomes a held breath. At The Switch, orange and yellow can coexist without blending into a new symbolic identity. During the insult, ordinary room light gradually becomes more legible as the song loses its shelter. In the climax, use match cuts and musical counterpoint across the Buffalo camp and New York premiere, preserving travel geography. The finale connects their lives without falsely putting all eight in the same place at once.

Create an original, immediately legible theatrical wordmark reading exactly “BOUNCE”, spelled B–O–U–N–C–E. Replace the letter O entirely with one round basketball. There must be no separate typographic O behind or beside the ball. Use heavy condensed sans-serif uppercase letters, warm off-white, on a flat near-black theatrical background. The basketball is a highly photorealistic orange-brown pebbled leather ball with familiar deep, dark rubber seam channels, matching a professional regulation basketball. Print an original Bounce wordmark in bold connected athletic script with an ordinary letter o on the leather, above an original vertical navy-and-orange badge of the fictional protagonist leaping to dunk. No manufacturer or league logos. Keep this small on-ball lettering distinct from the main title. Its visible diameter equals the visible cap height of the surrounding letters and it sits on their baseline. Tight, deliberate optical spacing. The result must read BOUNCE instantly at both poster scale and 160-pixel thumbnail size. One title only. Proportionate orange neon around every letter and the basketball rim and seams; no extra balls, no additional headlines, no sports-team badge, no lamp, no borrowed studio logo. Crisp typographic lettering paired with a photographic leather ball and consistent upper-left lighting. Leave sufficient quiet margin around the complete mark.

## Standalone image-generation prompt: theatrical poster

Create a premium original 2:3 vertical theatrical poster for an adult contemporary New York ensemble musical and feature film titled exactly “BOUNCE”. At the top, a bold, immediately legible condensed uppercase wordmark in warm off-white: B + an orange basketball replacing the O + UNCE. The basketball must have recognizable black seams, match the cap height of the letters and occupy the O's actual place. Do not add a second O. At the bottom, set exactly “EVERY FALL HAS A RHYTHM.” No other text, review quotes, awards or billing.

Center Isaiah “Zay” Reed, a fictional Black male basketball hopeful, age 26, 6'8”, in ordinary worn practice clothing with an orange basketball. He is athletic, playful and human, not superheroic. A concentrated yellow overhead light gives him a readable face and grounded presence. Around him, show exactly seven other adult principals in distinct, legible environmental zones: Lena, a ballet-trained exotic dancer authoring a sensual, disciplined rehearsal phrase in orange; Dani, a comedian working a microphone in blue; Andre as the commanding drag performer Velvet Saint in violet; Ben, an actor rehearsing a marked script in red; Jude, a lead guitarist working at an amplifier in green; Iris, a painter making an unfinished portrait in indigo; Morgan, a composer with keyboard and handwritten score pages in pearl white. Each is absorbed in their own work rather than gazing at Isaiah. All eight figures must remain inside the final crop.

Use dark theatrical space grounded in a real nighttime New York court and neighborhood workrooms, tactile pavement and timber, fine cinematic grain, rich charcoal shadows and restrained basketball-orange details. Maintain eight distinct pools of light with neutral face fill, never a generalized rainbow haze. Leave breathing room for the exact title and tagline. Emotional tone: funny, sensual, bruised and hopeful. Avoid mystical chakra symbols, fantasy powers, halos shaped as religious icons, championship trophies, celebrity likenesses, additional people, extra basketballs and watermarks. Original concept casting only.
